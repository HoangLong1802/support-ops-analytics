# Business requirements

The support operations team needs reliable visibility into demand, service commitments, customer experience and daily handling capacity. The analysis should identify cohorts to review and quantify their contribution before recommending operational changes.

Stakeholders: support leadership, operations managers, team leads, workforce planners and reporting analysts.

Business questions: When and through which channels does demand arrive? Which categories and priorities contribute service breaches? What is still open at the snapshot? How do ownership, actual handling effort, case mix and productive capacity differ across agents and teams? What daily capacity would cover recent matching-weekday handling at an 85% planning utilization?

KPIs: ticket volume; first-response, resolution and overall SLA compliance; elapsed response/resolution durations; mean CSAT and survey response rate; Reopen Rate; snapshot backlog and age; handling hours; productive capacity; utilization; daily estimated FTE gap.

Analytical scope: local creation dates/hours, cohort mix, fixed-snapshot service outcomes, final-owner associations, actual-handler effort and retrospective daily workforce planning. SLA durations compare UTC instants; compliance excludes pending cases.

Data scope: five synthetic sources, 15,000 logical tickets before intentional quality defects, 18 agents across three teams, and 365 operational dates from 2025-10-01 through 2026-09-30. Snapshot: 2026-10-01 00:00 Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh.

Limitations: synthetic relationships cannot validate real company performance or causes. Snapshot data cannot reconstruct historical backlog or owner transfers. Reopen Rate is not FCR. Resolution time includes waiting and differs from handling time. Daily workforce data cannot determine exact hourly coverage. Quarantine and survey nonresponse affect representativeness.
